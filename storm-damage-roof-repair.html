When property owners call about this

  • Missing, creased, lifted, or displaced shingles after high wind
  • New ceiling stains or attic moisture following a storm
  • Damage around ridge caps, vents, chimneys, skylights, or roof edges
  • Branches, debris, or impact marks on the roof or gutter system

How the repair-versus-replacement decision should be made

Not every storm call requires a complete roof replacement. A focused repair may make sense when damage is isolated and the surrounding system remains serviceable. Broader replacement becomes more likely when the storm exposes widespread age-related failure, repeated leak history, or damage across multiple roof planes.

Safety and scope limits

Stay off wet, icy, steep, or visibly damaged roofing. Photograph conditions from the ground or an interior window when safe, protect belongings below active leaks, and call emergency services for electrical hazards, structural movement, or immediate danger.

Common questions

What should I share when I call?

Share the property address, roof type if known, where the problem is visible, when it started, prior repair history, and safe photos. Those details help organize the first conversation.

Does every problem require full replacement?

No. The recommendation should follow the extent of damage, surrounding material condition, roof age, repair history, drainage, flashing, and whether the problem is isolated or system-wide.
