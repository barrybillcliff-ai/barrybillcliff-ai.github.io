Materials and brands

Roofing materials should be explained in terms of the roof problem, not just product names.

Modern Vision Roofing uses this page to help homeowners understand how shingles, metal roofing, EPDM, TPO, underlayment, flashing, ventilation, and drainage choices affect the finished roof system. The point is not to overwhelm the owner with brand names. The point is to make the options easier to compare.

Ask about materials

Built as a system

A roof is more than the shingle you see from the street.

Good roof planning includes shingles or panels, underlayment, ice and water protection, flashing, ventilation, ridge caps, gutters, and roofline drainage.

Core roofing material options

  • Asphalt shingles: common, practical, and cost-conscious for most homes.
  • Metal roofing: long-life option for selected homes, porches, and accents.
  • Flat / low-slope systems: useful for porches, additions, garages, and small commercial roofs where shingles are not appropriate.
  • Underlayment and ice/water protection: hidden materials that matter in New England weather.
  • Flashing: critical at chimneys, walls, valleys, skylights, and penetrations.
  • Ventilation: helps the roof system perform and can affect moisture, ice, and shingle life.

What matters beyond the brand label

The visible top layer is only one part of the result. A good roof system also depends on the underlayment, ice-and-water protection, flashing details, ventilation strategy, drip edge, ridge treatment, roof transitions, and how the gutters move water off the structure.

How homeowners should compare material options

The right comparison usually comes down to roof slope, weather exposure, repairability, budget, appearance, and how long the owner expects to keep the property. The strongest estimate conversations explain those tradeoffs clearly instead of acting like one system fits every house.
