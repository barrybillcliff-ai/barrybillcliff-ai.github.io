Replacement roofing

Roof replacement should solve the whole roof system, not just swap old shingles for new ones.

If the roof is aging out, leaking in multiple places, or no longer worth patching, Modern Vision Roofing helps property owners compare full replacement options with clearer language around materials, flashing, ventilation, drainage, cleanup, and what the finished system should include.

When replacement makes sense

A full replacement is usually the right call when the roof has repeat leaks in multiple areas, visible wear across large sections, aging flashing details, brittle shingles, exposed underlayment, or repair history that keeps growing without restoring real confidence in the roof.

  • Architectural asphalt shingle replacement
  • Storm or wind-damaged roof replacement planning
  • Underlayment, flashing, ventilation, and drip-edge review
  • Gutter and roofline concerns checked at the same time

What a full replacement estimate should explain

The proposal should make it clear what is being torn off, what accessories are being replaced, how ventilation is being handled, what flashing details are included, whether decking repairs are possible, and how the cleanup plan works. Homeowners should not have to guess what the number includes.

Material direction matters

Some homes are best served by architectural shingles. Others push owners toward metal accents, drainage upgrades, or better low-slope transitions over porches and additions. A professional roofing site should talk through those choices instead of hiding them behind one-line product labels.

When to stop spending on patches

If new leaks keep showing up in different areas, the roof is already old, and every repair creates one more exception instead of restoring confidence, the replacement discussion needs to happen directly. That is not upselling. That is helping the owner stop buying the same temporary answer over and over.
